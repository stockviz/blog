## Root cause (turnover is the killer)

| Series | flips/yr | % long | % flat | % short | full Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tdqn_art | 177 | 64% | 8% | 28% | -2.08 |
| tdqn_real | 222 | 53% | 0% | 47% | -3.49 |
| random | 226 | 33% | 33% | 34% | -4.66 |

At the house 25bps/flip, 177-222 flips/yr is a **44-55%/yr cost drag** —
bigger than any plausible alpha. The random floor confirms the math
(-46%/yr). The DSR reward is per-step local, tiny in magnitude (~1e-3), and
ε-greedy exploration injects high-cost flip transitions into the replay —
the agent learns "churn less than random", never "hold". This is the KB's
own turnover warning (KB 08 §8.2: costs dominate net Sharpe).

**Positive sub-finding (feeds R1.4):** tdqn_art beat tdqn_real in every
window (full -2.08 vs -3.49) — the S4 artificial-trajectory training
reduced overfit to the 2000-2014 regime, exactly as Théate argued. The
mechanism works; the reward/cost balance is broken.

## Fix agenda (R1.2 — the plan's "stop and fix" gate)

1. **Reward sweep (R1.2):** per-step differential Sharpe -> episode-level
   Sharpe (S4's actual recipe) and churn-penalized PnL (`a*r - c|Δa| -
   λ·turnover`). The DSR-as-immediate-reward variant is the suspect.
2. **Cost realism:** the index benchmark should be compared at
   index-futures costs (~2.5-5bps), with 25bps as the equity-book stress
   case — report the cost level at which TDQN breaks even vs B&H.
3. **Action persistence:** explicit hold prior / gradual position changes
   (target-position actions with capped per-day delta) to break the
   flip-churn equilibrium.
4. Re-run the R1.1 honesty check after each change — the gate is still:
   beat MA-cross net of costs before any variant spending.
