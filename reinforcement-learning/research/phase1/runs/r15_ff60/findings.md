## Synthesis (FF60 / wider-universe verdict)

1. The pnl-reward TDQN's default behavior is **drift-betting**: it learns the
   training window's best CONSTANT direction (short in the 2015-19 small-cap
   bear, long in a bull) unless the training is made regime-neutral.
2. **Drift-free artificial trajectories + per-stock 1/vol reward scaling +
   long-only actions + a decile-stratified training slice** stabilise the
   shared agent (no degenerate corners), but the learned conditional timing
   adds no edge over classical rules on the wide universe at 5bps: book
   +0.46 vs MA +1.69 and B&H +1.57; agent beats its own MA on only 16% of
   names.
3. **Re-reading the top-50 result**: its +1.21 book was largely the same
   drift-betting mechanism paying off — the train window (mega-cap bull
   2015-19) and test window (2020+ bull) were the SAME regime. The agent was
   92-97% long, i.e., mostly B&H with modest timing. The cross-sectional RL
   edge claim from R1.5-top50 must therefore be tempered: conditional
   timing skill was never demonstrated; regime alignment was.
4. What REMAINS genuinely positive across all runs: the agent de-risks
   (attempt-4 MaxDD 13.2% vs MA 15.7% / B&H 25.3% at far lower exposure)
   and it never blows up per-name. But earning while de-risked requires
   predictive state, which momentum z-features do not provide in this
   setup.
5. Honest recommendation per the plan: wide-universe single-name {-1,0,1}
   TDQN at 5bps does NOT beat classical rules in 2020+. Candidates that
   could change this: (a) stronger cross-sectional state (breadth deciles,
   sector-relative momentum, the R1.3 VIX/p_off regime features per stock),
   (b) continuous allocation (PPO) so de-risking is partial instead of
   binary, (c) 25bps realism check, or (d) accept the negative and move to
   Phase 2 intraday where classical rules are weak.
