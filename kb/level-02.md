## Volume Clock, an Introduction

This article introduces the Volume Clock, a method of aggregating financial data by volume rather than chronological time. Traditional charts use time intervals, but volume indicates the speed of information arrival. By dividing the trading day into equal volume buckets, traders transform chrono-time into event-based time. The piece highlights three major statistical advantages of this approach: it removes intra-session seasonal effects, partially restores Normality and the Independent and Identically Distributed (IID) assumption, and solves issues related to random and asynchronous transactions in high-frequency data. It also explains the U-shaped pattern of trading turnover throughout the day, showing that a volume clock automatically adjusts the sampling frequency to match the actual information flow. This foundational concept bridges the gap between standard time-based charts and advanced high-frequency trading paradigms.

This article analyzes the quarterly Assets Under Management (AUM) statistics for mutual funds published by AMFI. It highlights that the top five players, including HDFC and ICICI, consistently dominate market share, while a large majority of funds and schemes hold very small percentages of the total AUM. Furthermore, the text examines the emergence of direct plans, noting their growth in equity AUM and specific impacts on smaller funds and distributors, providing foundational insights into mutual fund industry structures and market concentration.
