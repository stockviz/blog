This lack of correlation forms the foundational basis for traditional portfolio allocations, as combining these two distinct asset classes results in lower overall portfolio volatility due to diversification mathematics. The text introduces charts highlighting how the asset characteristics differ and provides context on why blending them is an effective strategy for managing investment risk. The concepts require a basic understanding of asset classes, correlation, and portfolio diversification principles to comprehend how mixing uncorrelated investments helps stabilize overall portfolio performance during market fluctuations.

## Article

Stocks and Bonds are not correlated. They are not negatively correlated. And neither are they positively correlated. One doesn’t “zig” when the other “zags.” This is exactly why portfolio allocations start with stocks and bonds – the diversification math works on uncorrelated asset classes.
