## Summary

This document explores the challenges of making investment decisions under market uncertainty, illustrating the psychological and structural difficulties through the historical performance and drawdowns of stocks like TCS and Reliance Infrastructure. It emphasizes that most retail investors fail to achieve advertised long-term returns because they panic and sell during market drops or fail to re-enter at the right time. To counter emotional decision-making, the text advocates for a disciplined, systematic investment process that relies on pre-selected parameters, defined holding periods, and rules-based portfolio exits. Finally, it introduces StockViz Themes as a technological wrapper designed to help investors practically implement these strategies and maintain objectivity across various market conditions.

## Article

TCS has been a tremendous wealth creator. Between 2004-8-25 and now, its has turned in a cumulative return of 1164% with an IRR of 28.35% during that period. So you should just “buy right and sit tight”, yeah? But before you hang that on your wall, let me take you back to Jan 2007. TCS started sliding on global worries and did stop until losing 65.52% of its value. It took 458 days to make a bottom and another 249 days to climb back up from it. If you think the meltdown in 2007/8 was a once-in-a-lifetime affair, then let me point out that the second worst drawdown for TCS was 27.40% back in 2006. And before that, 24% in 2005. In my experience, 99% of the investors out there would have dumped the stock. That 28.35% IRR is wishful thinking for most investors because not only would they have dumped the stock at maximum pain (peak drawdown) but they would have failed to get back into the stock as well. But does this mean you should never sell a stock? Have a look at Reliance Infrastructure as a counter example. It is yet to recover from the 87% drawdown in 2008! It has been a value destroyer since early 2008. So does this mean that you should have a “stop loss”? How should you manage whiplash? How do you keep track of all the reasons you bought a stock and if it still fits that criteria? If you sell a stock, what do you replace it with? The key to making decisions under uncertainty is to have a process.
