## Summary

This document explores the relationship between stock market capitalization deciles and small-cap mutual fund portfolios. It explains how sorting stocks by free-float market capitalization creates StockViz Deciles, where market activity and liquidity are heavily concentrated in the top deciles. The text highlights a significant scale challenge for small-cap fund managers due to wide bid-offer spreads in lower deciles, which results in funds concentrating their portfolios above the fourth decile and occasionally holding mega-cap stocks to manage asset under management growth. The document serves as an applied analysis of fund portfolio mandates versus practical execution constraints in equity markets.

## Article

When you sort the universe of stocks in descending order of their free-float market caps and divide them into 10 sets, you end up with StockViz Deciles. If you were to plot the dispersion of market-caps within deciles, here’s how it would look: Most of the activity in the markets are in the first 3-4 deciles. Liquidity, as measured by the bid/offer spread, trails off as the float drops: The wide bid/offers presents a scale challenge to small-cap fund managers. The hair-cut to NAV that they will have to take while crossing the spread is just too large. So most small-cap funds pull up: Notice how most of the portfolios is concentrated above the 4th decile. Now, contrast this to the NIFTY SMALLCAP 250 index: If the funds were to stay true to their small-cap moniker, they really shouldn’t be holding decile 0 (mega-cap) stocks. However, holding them seems to be the only way to scale AUM. If you care about whether a fund is sticking to its portfolio mandate, give our Overlap Tool a spin.
