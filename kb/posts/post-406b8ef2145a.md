# Ten years is a long time

- Difficulty level: 3
- Published: Wed, 15 Jul 2015 06:54:56 +0000
- Source: [https://stockviz.biz/2015/07/15/ten-years-is-a-long-time/](https://stockviz.biz/2015/07/15/ten-years-is-a-long-time/)

## Summary

This article examines the historical returns of the Nifty and Defty indices over various time horizons, highlighting that short- to medium-term holding periods often yield negative or low single-digit returns, which can challenge investor discipline. It points out that negative returns occurred in a significant portion of the past twenty-four years, and dollar-denominated investors faced even harsher conditions. The text stresses that meaningful equity returns and the true benefits of long-term investing typically only emerge when holding periods extend to seven years and beyond. Furthermore, it addresses the disconnect between long-term portfolio strategies and the short-term focus often promoted by financial media and advisors. Ultimately, the piece emphasizes that successful long-term investing requires the psychological discipline to ignore short-term fluctuations and maintain commitment across decades, despite the difficulty of doing so.

## Article

Yearly Nifty and Defty Returns When you look at the last 24 years of returns, the NIFTY gave negative returns for 8 years – 1/3rd of the time. Holding period matters The most frequent advice given to new equity investors is that they should at least have a 3-year horizon. The problem with this is that there have been 7 periods where cumulative 3-year returns have been negative or single digits. This sort of advice does nothing to mentally prepare the investor to stay disciplined. In fact, dollar investors (NRIs, etc.) have had it rougher. It is only when you look at 7-years and beyond that you start seeing the kind of returns that begin to make sense.
