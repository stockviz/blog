## Summary

This article examines rolling 5-year monthly return correlations between the MSCI India index and various other country indices to evaluate the effectiveness of international equity diversification. Using comparative analysis, it highlights that Jordan exhibits the lowest correlation while Hong Kong demonstrates the highest correlation with India. The discussion investigates whether constructing a balanced 50/50 portfolio between India and Jordan or India and Hong Kong helps mitigate drawdowns and volatility. The findings suggest that diversification within the same asset class, such as global equities, does not necessarily reduce drawdowns or guarantee higher returns. Instead, cross-border equity diversification serves primarily as a safeguard against single-country concentration risk and hindsight mistakes, contrasting with cross-asset diversification strategies like combining stocks and bonds.

## Article

All stocks are correlated to one-another. In times of crisis, these correlations explode higher. The same is true for country indices. For example, if you look at the rolling 5-year monthly return correlations between the MSCI INDIA index and other country indices, Jordan is the least correlated and Hong Kong is the most correlated.
