## Summary

This article provides a quantitative evaluation of mutual funds using multiple risk metrics, including the sharpe ratio, bear-beta, information ratio, draw-down depth, and draw-down length, covering the period from January 2010 to May 2015. It highlights the vast number of choices available to investors and aims to simplify decision-making by ranking the top 10 funds across different analytical categories. The post discusses the implications of various performance and risk indicators, such as single digit IRRs and shorter bounce-backs as signs of high-quality portfolios, concluding that investors should weigh appropriate metrics based on their individual risk appetites.

## Article

Introduction Mutual fund investors are faced with a zillion choices in the marketplace. At last count, there were more than 5300 different schemes that an investor could choose from. When confronted with such a large number of choices, investors either spiral into an “analysis paralysis” mode and end up doing nothing or blindly invest in whatever their broker recommends – both these paths lead to situations that are injurious to the investor’s long-term financial health. In this post, we try to simplify the choices in front of the investor by ranking the top 10 funds based these risk metrics: sharpe ratio, bear-beta, information ratio, draw-down depth and draw-down length between Jan-2010 and May-2015.
