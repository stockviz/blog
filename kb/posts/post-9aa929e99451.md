## Summary

This article discusses momentum investing alongside value investing as the two enduring anomalies in finance that challenge the Efficient Market Hypothesis by producing out-sized market returns. While value investing receives substantial media attention, momentum investing is frequently overlooked and results in portfolios being underweight in momentum strategies. The primary drawback of momentum investing is its potential for severe draw-downs when momentum stocks experience sharp declines, causing mutual funds to avoid the strategy due to volatility concerns. However, individual investors who can tolerate volatility face fewer constraints and can benefit from momentum approaches. A comparison between momentum investing and the ICICI Prudential Value Discovery Fund from January 1, 2014, to June 18, 2015, demonstrates that momentum achieved a higher internal rate of return. Investors are advised to allocate capital gradually based on their risk tolerance, starting around ten percent and scaling up to their personal limit.

## Article

The Two Anomalies in Finance Momentum and Value remain the two “anomalies” in finance. The Efficient Market Hypothesis cannot explain why value investing, where investors pick up “under-priced” stocks, and momentum investing, where investors bet on stocks that have already run up, give out-sized returns compared to the rest of the market. After all, aren’t markets supposed to discover the “right” price and negate these effects? Investing in Momentum Value investors get a lot of face-time in media – people like to hear about stocks that are “hidden gems” that can suddenly come alive and give out-sized returns. However, very little is spoken about momentum investing. This results in investment portfolios that are underweight momentum. The problem with momentum investing are the large draw-downs. When momentum stocks tank, they do so spectacularly. The draw-down keeps away most mutual funds from seriously pursuing this strategy: a) they can’t get out easily, and b) if they show too much volatility, investors will revolt. But individual investors don’t have these constraints if they learn to embrace volatility.
