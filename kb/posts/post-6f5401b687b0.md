## Summary

This document compares the observed historical volatility of the iShares MSCI India ETF (INDA) with the S&P 500 ETF (SPY). It discusses how dollar-denominated emerging market exchange-traded funds exhibit greater volatility compared to broader U.S. market indexes like the S&P 500. Additionally, the text references historical volatility analysis of the NIFTY index across various periods, exploring density plots, distribution characteristics, and mean-reversion expectations in market volatility. The material connects single-country exposure strategies in India with broader portfolio risk and volatility metrics, providing applied analytical context for investment and trading strategies facing emerging market currency and equity fluctuations.

## Article

The iShares MSCI India ETF (INDA) tracks the MSCI India Total Return Index, representing about 85% of the Indian stock market. As a follow up to our earlier post on the historical volatility of the NIFTY , we thought we’ll compare the volatilities of INDA and SPY, the S&P 500 ETF. As expected, a dollar denominated emerging market ETF is more volatile than the S&P. File this away in your brain attic.
