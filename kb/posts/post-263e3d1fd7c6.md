## Summary

This article explores the application of trend-following systems to the Indian commodity markets, specifically focusing on the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX). It highlights that trading activity is highly concentrated between 6 PM and 9 PM, primarily in volatile commodities like silver and natural gas. The author suggests that because not all listed commodities trade equally, their inclusion in a multi-asset trend-following portfolio requires careful measurement of their distinct activity profiles and effects on overall correlation. To understand this piece, readers should already possess foundational knowledge of trend-following strategies, asset class correlations, and basic commodity market mechanics. The article serves as a practical application of portfolio diversification methods, evaluating how specific, highly volatile Indian commodity tickers might alter the risk-return dynamics of an existing systematic trading approach.

## Article

Typically, trend-following systems span multiple asset classes in order to reduce correlations. There could be a case for applying a trend-following system over equities and commodities in India. However, not all commodities trade and activity profiles can be vastly different. It appears that most of the trading activity at the MCX occurs between 6pm and 9pm. Not all listed commodities trade… … and most of the activity centers around silver and natural gas – two of the most volatile commodities. It maybe worthwhile to add at least some of these tickers into the mix and measure their effect on trend-following portfolios.
