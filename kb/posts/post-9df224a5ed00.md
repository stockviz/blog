# Two Relative Strength Index (RSI) Strategies that Do Not Work

- Difficulty level: 3
- Published: Sun, 17 Aug 2014 07:13:57 +0000
- Source: [https://stockviz.biz/2014/08/17/two-relative-strength-index-rsi-strategies-work/](https://stockviz.biz/2014/08/17/two-relative-strength-index-rsi-strategies-work/)

## Summary

This article evaluates two naive Relative Strength Index trading strategies using overbought thresholds and moving averages across indices like NIFTY and CNX BANK. The analysis compares these RSI-based rules against a standard buy-and-hold approach and a 200-day simple moving average strategy. The findings demonstrate that exiting positions when the RSI indicates overbought conditions consistently underperforms simpler baseline strategies. The text concludes that these specific implementation methods fail to generate sufficient gains to compensate for losses, ultimately proving to be ineffective relative to basic passive holding and trend-following methods.

## Article

Definition from StockCharts: A naive implementation of it would be exit when RSI > 70. Lets see how it worked out vs. a simple buy-and-hold. A simple B&H strategy was miles ahead of one that exited the market whenever RSI indicated “overbought.” How about being long only when the index is an uptrend (> 200-day SMA) and RSI is not ‘overbought’ (< 70)? Using RSI was a net negative – it under-performed both a buy-and-hold and a SMA-200 strategy. Would it work on other indices? NIFTY: NO CNX BANK: NO Conclusion The naive RSI strategies described above are money losers. The gains are nowhere near enough to compensate for the losses.
