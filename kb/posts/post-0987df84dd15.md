## Summary

The article evaluates the effectiveness of trend-following strategies, specifically Simple Moving Average (SMA) filters, applied to both Indian and US bond markets. For Indian bonds, the analysis concludes that a straightforward buy-and-hold approach is superior once transaction costs and taxes are accounted for, negating any trend-following benefits. In the US market, the study applies 100-day SMA scenarios to popular bond ETFs, including TLT, IEF, and SHY. While the filter shows some pre-cost benefits for these US instruments, the after-cost advantages are questionable and likely insufficient to justify the strategy. The research utilizes recent historical data to complement earlier studies, and the authors provide the complete code and visual charts on GitHub for further examination and replication.

## Article

Does trend following work on bonds? According to alphaarchitect, it should. However, they use data going back to 1928 and we wanted to look at something more recent. Also, we wanted to check if it worked for Indian bonds? For Indian bonds, you are better off buying and holding. Once you consider transaction costs and taxes, there is no benefit. For US, we ran the same SMA scenarios on the TLT (20+), IEF (7-10), SHY (1-3) and AGG etfs. There is some benefit to applying a 100-day SMA filter on the first three. However, the after-cost benefits are questionable. Code and charts on github.
