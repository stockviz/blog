# ETFs vs. Indices

## Summary

This article analyzes the effectiveness of Indian Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) in tracking their underlying indices by examining statistical measures such as kurtosis and skewness in daily returns. It compares specific funds like NIFTYBEES, PSUBNKBEES, and JUNIORBEES with their respective indices, CNX Nifty, CNX PSU Bank, and CNX Nifty Junior. The discussion highlights how tracking error and asset management fees manifest differently in liquid versus less liquid and higher-fee ETFs, showing minimal impact on cumulative returns for efficient funds but significant deviations for others. The piece concludes by observing that current market dynamics are unlikely to shift in the short term due to limited pressure from investors or asset management firms.

## Article

ETFs (Exchange Traded Funds) offer investors a convenient way to gain exposure to a particular index. Since these funds are not actively managed, they are measured by their how cheap they are (in terms of asset management fees) and their tracking error. Before we begin, some key terms: Kurtosis Kurtosis is a measure of “peakedness” of a distribution. For a normal distribution, Kurtosis is 3. Positive excess Kurtosis indicates fat tails while negative indicates peakedness. Skew Skewness is a measure of asymmetry of a distribution. Positive skew indicates a long right tail while a negative skew indicates a long left tail. How effective have Indian ETFs been? Lets pull up some histograms and see for ourselves.
